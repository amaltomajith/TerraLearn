Reference: IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 5 (Food, Fibre, Ecosystem Products), Lobell et al. heat stress crop research

Heat stress in crops occurs when temperatures exceed the physiological optimum during critical growth stages, particularly flowering and grain fill. Even a few days of extreme heat at the right moment can devastate yield disproportionately to what season-average temperatures would predict.

For wheat, the critical heat stress threshold during the grain-filling period is generally accepted as a daily maximum above 30°C or a mean temperature above 25°C. At temperatures exceeding 35°C during anthesis (flowering), pollen viability drops sharply — pollen grains desiccate in hot dry air, and fertilisation fails even if photosynthesis is intact. Each degree of mean temperature above 25°C during grain fill reduces wheat yield by approximately 3–8% depending on variety and water availability. In the Indo-Gangetic Plain, late-sown wheat (December planting or later) is particularly exposed to terminal heat stress in March–April when grain is developing.

For rice, heat stress thresholds differ by stage. During the pollination and early grain fill stage (usually around heading), sustained daytime temperatures above 35°C impair pollen germination on the stigma, leading to sterile spikelets and yield loss. Night-time temperatures are also critical for rice: nights above 26°C increase dark respiration, burning photosynthate that would otherwise accumulate as grain starch. Research in tropical Asia suggests each 1°C increase in minimum night temperature reduces rice yields by 6–10%. Kharif (monsoon) rice in India is increasingly exposed to high night temperatures due to warming trends.

For maize, the critical threshold for pollen shedding and silk emergence is around 35°C during flowering. Maize is particularly vulnerable to 2–3 day heat events during the narrow 1–2 week window of pollination. For cotton and soybean, sustained temperatures above 38–40°C during flowering reduce fruit/pod set.

Management options for heat stress include early planting (shifting sensitive stages away from peak heat months), selecting heat-tolerant varieties, and supplemental irrigation before/during critical stages (which cools the canopy and increases humidity around flowers).
